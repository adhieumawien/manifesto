Proposed political manifesto

A stronger voice.
A safer community.
A better future.

Samuel Adhieu Mawien Arou · Aspirant for Member of Parliament, Constituency No. 19, Awul and Manalor Payams, Tonj North County, Warrap State.

Public office is a responsibility to serve, not a privilege to enjoy.

Preamble

South Sudan faces serious development challenges, including conflict, economic hardship, limited infrastructure, weak access to public services and recurring humanitarian emergencies. In Warrap State, insecurity, cattle-related violence, climate shocks, food insecurity and barriers to education and healthcare place heavy pressure on families.

Awul and Manalor deserve effective representation that translates community concerns into practical action. This manifesto sets out a proposed agenda for peace, justice, education, health, livelihoods, inclusive development and accountable leadership. It is a commitment to advocacy, legislative work, public oversight and partnership, not a claim that every project is already funded or approved.

Vision and mission

Vision

To build a peaceful, secure, educated, healthy, productive and united Constituency No. 19 where people can live with dignity, access essential services and participate meaningfully in decisions affecting their future.

Mission

To represent Awul and Manalor effectively through lawmaking, government oversight, community consultation and advocacy for equitable public investment, access to justice and stronger partnerships.

1. Peace, security and civilian protection

Peace is the foundation of development. Insecurity disrupts education, trade, livestock production, humanitarian assistance and family stability. Women and children often bear a disproportionate burden when violence displaces families or separates them from their livelihoods.

  • Promote regular peace consultations involving traditional leaders, women, youth, cattle keepers, religious institutions and neighbouring communities.
  • Advocate for early-warning and response arrangements and stronger coordination with relevant security institutions.
  • Support peaceful and lawful resolution of cattle theft, revenge attacks, abductions, grazing disputes and water-access conflicts.
  • Advocate for impartial investigations and lawful action against serious crimes, including killings and sexual violence.
  • Promote civilian protection, human rights education and safe, voluntary return and reintegration for displaced families.

2. Education for every child

Every child deserves an opportunity to learn. Poverty, insecurity, school costs, long distances, early marriage and inadequate learning facilities can prevent children from attending school consistently.

  • Advocate for classroom rehabilitation, school access and better learning conditions based on assessed needs.
  • Engage education authorities on teacher recruitment, deployment, attendance and professional development.
  • Promote girls' retention, menstrual hygiene support and prevention of early and forced marriage.
  • Advocate for school feeding and targeted assistance for vulnerable children where needs and funding justify it.
  • Support adult literacy, catch-up education, technical training and digital literacy.
  • Promote safe schools free from exploitation, abuse and discrimination.

3. Healthcare, nutrition and human dignity

Distance, transport costs, shortages of trained health workers and limited medical supplies make preventable illnesses dangerous for rural families. Maternal health, newborn care, childhood illness and malnutrition require particular attention.

  • Advocate for a health-service assessment in Farasika, Awul and other underserved areas.
  • Seek improved primary healthcare, maternal and newborn services, immunisation and referral arrangements.
  • Promote health outreach for remote settlements and mobile populations.
  • Advocate for screening and treatment of acute malnutrition among children under five and pregnant and breastfeeding women.
  • Promote safe water, sanitation, hygiene and community health education.

4. Food security, agriculture and resilient livelihoods

Humanitarian food assistance remains essential for families facing acute hunger, but it should be complemented by longer-term measures that strengthen household production and resilience.

  • Advocate for timely, impartial and needs-based humanitarian assistance.
  • Promote agricultural extension, improved seeds, appropriate tools and climate-resilient farming.
  • Support livestock vaccination, veterinary outreach and animal-health services.
  • Encourage grain storage, produce aggregation, local processing and reduced post-harvest losses.
  • Advocate for livelihood recovery for families whose homes, farms or livestock have been lost.

5. Roads, water, sanitation and infrastructure

  • Advocate for technical assessment and phased improvement of feeder roads connecting communities to schools, health facilities, markets and administrative centres.
  • Promote safe and reliable community water points with clear maintenance arrangements.
  • Advocate for improved sanitation in schools, health facilities and public places.
  • Encourage solar energy and suitable renewable-energy solutions for essential services where feasible.
  • Promote public disclosure of approved projects, budgets, contractors and implementation status where legally available.

6. Youth empowerment, employment and entrepreneurship

  • Advocate for vocational training in locally relevant trades, agriculture, livestock management and information technology.
  • Promote apprenticeships linking young people with skilled tradespeople and businesses.
  • Encourage youth cooperatives, savings groups, financial literacy and enterprise development.
  • Advocate for fair access to available training and employment opportunities.
  • Support youth participation in peacebuilding, sports, environmental conservation and community service.

7. Women, children and social inclusion

  • Promote women's participation in community decisions, peace processes and public consultations.
  • Advocate for protection and referral services for survivors of gender-based violence.
  • Support measures to prevent early and forced marriage, sexual violence and child exploitation.
  • Advocate for accessible services for persons with disabilities, displaced people, returnees and extremely vulnerable households.
  • Promote confidential complaint and referral systems that protect people from retaliation.

8. Rule of law, access to justice and human rights

Justice must not depend on wealth, family connections, political influence or the ability to mobilise armed supporters.

  • Advocate for improved access to police, courts, legal assistance and civil documentation services.
  • Promote legal awareness on family rights, land disputes, child protection and criminal justice.
  • Encourage cooperation between formal justice institutions and customary authorities while upholding fundamental rights and applicable law.
  • Support fair, timely and impartial handling of complaints and investigations.
  • Promote civic education on citizens' rights, responsibilities and lawful engagement with public institutions.

9. Local economic development and markets

  • Advocate for better market access and transport connections between rural producers and trading centres.
  • Promote agricultural and livestock value chains, producer groups and cooperatives.
  • Encourage small businesses in retail trade, food processing, construction, transport and repair services.
  • Support women's and youth-led enterprises, savings groups and financial literacy.
  • Promote responsible management of land, water and other natural resources.

10. Accountable governance and stronger representation

  • Hold regular consultations with elders, chiefs, women, youth, teachers, health workers, farmers, cattle keepers and traders.
  • Maintain a constituency issue register and an accessible way for residents to raise concerns.
  • Raise constituency priorities through appropriate parliamentary debates, committee work and budget discussions.
  • Publish periodic updates on activities, issues raised, commitments secured and matters awaiting action.
  • Promote transparent, needs-based allocation of public resources and lawful reporting of suspected misuse.
  • Represent all constituents fairly, including those with different political opinions.

Humanitarian response: protect lives and restore dignity

People affected by conflict, displacement, hunger, malnutrition, drought or flooding must receive assistance according to assessed needs, without discrimination or political conditions.

  • Advocate for rapid needs assessments and coordinated emergency response.
  • Promote safe humanitarian access and protection of civilians and humanitarian workers.
  • Support coordination between government authorities, humanitarian agencies and communities.
  • Link emergency assistance with livelihood recovery, agricultural support and resilience-building.
  • Encourage transparent targeting, community feedback and accessible complaints mechanisms.

Priorities for Awul and Manalor

The initial constituency agenda will focus on health-service access in Farasika and underserved areas; girls' education and school retention; peace and civilian protection; feeder roads and connectivity; household livelihoods; and safe water and sanitation. These priorities should be refined through consultation with residents of both payams and technical assessments of needs, feasibility and available resources.

Warrap State and national engagement

Beyond the constituency, the proposed agenda includes coordinated peacebuilding, equitable development investment, stronger essential services, food security and livestock development, women's and youth participation, and accountable governance. At the national level, the candidate will support constitutional processes, legislative oversight, human rights, rural development and constructive cooperation between government institutions.

Turning the manifesto into action

First 100 days: listen and set priorities

  • Consult residents across Awul and Manalor.
  • Document priority issues and identify urgent concerns.
  • Meet relevant authorities and communicate an initial work plan.

First year: advocate and engage

  • Raise constituency priorities through appropriate parliamentary channels.
  • Advocate for inclusion of feasible projects in relevant plans and budgets.
  • Convene public meetings to review progress and unresolved issues.

Ongoing: monitor and report

  • Provide regular updates on parliamentary work and constituency engagements.
  • Track priority projects and explain delays or barriers.
  • Invite public feedback and revise priorities when evidence or circumstances change.

Important: These are proposed working milestones. Implementation depends on parliamentary procedures, available resources, institutional cooperation and the security environment.

Integrity and public accountability

The candidate's proposed standards are honesty, accessibility, fairness, transparency, responsible use of public resources, respect for institutions, non-violence, inclusion and service to the public. A Member of Parliament can advocate, legislate and scrutinise government performance, but cannot personally guarantee funding or directly deliver every service. Where implementation rests with other institutions, the role is to engage them, seek explanations and monitor progress.

Final pledge

I, Samuel Adhieu Mawien Arou, seek the opportunity to serve Constituency No. 19 with humility and a clear understanding of public responsibility.

I pledge to listen before making decisions, consult before advancing major priorities, advocate persistently for our people and report honestly on progress. I will work for peace where there is conflict, justice where there is unfairness, education where children face barriers, healthcare where services are lacking, and opportunity where poverty limits potential.

I offer this manifesto not as a promise that every challenge will disappear overnight, but as a public commitment to principled leadership, persistent advocacy, responsible oversight and measurable progress.

A peaceful constituency. A stronger community. A more accountable government. A better future for every generation.

Samuel Adhieu Mawien Arou
Aspirant for Member of Parliament, Constituency No. 19
